A 404 worth landing on

Taking the TryHackMe page as the reference. What makes it work is not the size of the number — it is that the characters it is built from are the site's own subject. Ones and zeroes on a hacking site say where you are as well as what went wrong.

PeerFlow's version is the mark. The number is built out of about a hundred and twenty copies of the PeerFlow logo, one per cell — no mascot to own and draw forever, and nothing borrowed. I tried code punctuation first, on the theory that { } < > / ( ) is the one alphabet eight learning paths share; it did not read, because those glyphs all carry different amounts of ink and the eye never joins them into a stroke. 0 and 1 work for TryHackMe because they are one width, one height, and nothing hangs off them.

The graphic is the same in one and two. The decision is the ground it sits on: full-bleed dark with no site chrome, like the reference, or the site's own light shell keeping the header, the footer and the did you mean line. Three is the option that came out of PeerFlow rather than out of the reference.

1 · The field, dark and full-bleed

shipped, with two changes

The reference read straight across: no header, no footer, the number filling the screen, one sentence and two ways out. For: the version with impact — the whole viewport is the graphic, and the mark reads at this size in a way it never does at 26px in a nav bar. Against: it would be the only dark page on the public site, so the site changes colour under somebody who has just mistyped an address; dropping the header drops the nav, so the only ways out are the two we thought of; and there is nowhere on it for the did you mean line, which is the part that actually rescues the people who typed /privacy.

What shipped: this, with the two objections answered rather than accepted. The reference keeps its own site nav at the top of the page, so 404.html keeps PeerFlow's — the ways out are the site's, not two we picked. And the did you mean line sits above the button, hidden until the resolver has something true to put there, so an address that is close to nothing reads exactly like the panel below and an address that is close to something does not waste the click. The footer stays off: the page fills the viewport, as the reference does.

2 · The same field, on the site's own ground

not shipped

The same field, three steps down the green ramp so it holds on paper, sitting in the shell every other public page uses. For: it keeps the header and the footer, so the ways out are the site's own rather than two we picked; it keeps the did you mean line and the four destinations under it; and nothing about the site changes under somebody who has just mistyped an address. Against: a graphic inside a normal page is a smaller moment than a graphic that is the page — this reads as a good 404 rather than as one worth sending to somebody, which is the whole reason for asking.

3 · The empty room

not shipped

Not from the reference. The 404 rendered as a call nobody joined: the room's own palette, a waiting tile with the address on the name pill where a partner's name goes, and the call bar underneath with the ways out where the mic and camera controls sit. For: it is the only one of the three that could belong to no other product — a field of marks is a shape any site with a logo could make, an empty study room is not. Against: the joke lands once, it is dark for the same reason one is, and it has nowhere quiet to put the did you mean line.
