Tidying the dashboard

Three things you picked out: the page repeats itself, the right-hand column is too many small cards, and the visual system underneath it all is loose. This page fixes the third one outright — that is not a matter of taste — and then puts two real layouts next to each other for the first two. The calendar keeps the size and the place it has now.

1. The visual system

Counted, not guessed. assets/app.css today declares 34 distinct font sizes, 18 heading classes and 29 separate text-transform:uppercase rules. A type scale normally has six or seven steps. assets/tokens.css already defines one — app.css just never uses it.

One rule (.ch) replaces .cal-head, .bookp-h, .bh, .rail h3 and .pg-head.

One more, and it is the reason the page feels like it starts mid-sentence: every app page names itself except this one. Progress says “Progress”, People says “People on PeerFlow”. The dashboard's only <h1> is the green band, which currently reads “Loading your sessions…”. Both layouts below give it a title.

2. The page repeats itself

Tuesday's 8:00 session with Amir is on screen three times: as a sentence in the green band, as a block in the calendar, and as a row in “Sessions” under BOOKED. His name appears six times. The band and the calendar both earn their copy — one is the answer to “what now”, the other is the shape of the week. The list is the third telling, and it is the one that adds nothing.

So the card below the calendar stops being a list of everything and becomes only what is waiting on you: proposals to answer, and goals to close out. When nothing is waiting, it isn't there. Both layouts do this.

3. Two ways to fix the rail

Four cards — this week, your partner, standing slot, week 5 of 12 — each with its own internal structure. Below 1000px they collapse into four columns and the last one wraps “All 12 weeks” onto three lines. Both options cut them to one shape; they differ in where that shape goes.

Why A. The rail's four cards were never four things you do — they were four facts you glance at, and facts read better in a row than in a stack. Putting them in a strip also gives the calendar and the “needs you” card the full width, which is where the actual work happens, and it removes the 306px column that was breaking at 1000px. B is the smaller change: the grid stays exactly as it is and only the rail's contents are regrouped, so if the two-column shape is something you want to keep, B is the one.
