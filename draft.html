Report and block — how the control opens

The database half is merged and tested: block_person(), report_person(), five reasons, block-by-default, and policies that make a block actually stop a message, a partner request and a booking. What is left is the part you have to look at.

It has to work in two very different places. On app-person.html there is a whole page to use. Inside call.html there is live video underneath, and navigating anywhere at all drops the call. One control that works in both is worth more than two that each work in one.

And app.css already has an opinion, written beside the badge popup: “Everything else here tries not to be a modal; this is the single event worth stopping for.” Option B is a second modal. That is the argument, and it is a real one — reporting somebody may well be the second event worth stopping for.

All three are live. Click them.

A — an inline panel

Opens in the flow of the page and pushes what is below it down. No overlay, no dimming, nothing to trap focus inside, and it is the pattern .askbox already set for asking somebody to be your partner. The page stays a page.

C — reason first, everything else after

No form at all to begin with: five buttons, and pressing one sends the report and blocks them. The detail box and the "actually, don't block" choice appear afterwards, on a screen that can be walked away from because the thing is already done.

The argument for it is about the moment rather than the layout. Somebody reaching for this button has just been made uncomfortable, and that is not the state in which to be asked four questions. The argument against is that a one-press irreversible-feeling action is easy to hit by accident — met here by making it genuinely undoable for as long as the panel is open.

Where I'd land

B, the overlay sheet — and I think the modal rule bends here rather than breaks.

The rule in app.css is about interruption: don't stop somebody who is reading a page to show them something they did not ask for. This is the opposite. They pressed a button, they want the thing that follows, and the dimming is doing a job — on a call it is the only way to be legible over moving video, and it takes the other person's face off the screen while somebody types a sentence about them.

A is the better citizen on the profile page and falls apart on the call, where "inline" means a floating box over video that is a modal without the dimming — the costs of one with the benefits of neither.

C is the most humane and the one I would want if the whole thing lived on the call. But it needs an undo window to be safe, and an undo window is a fourth state on the one flow that must never be flaky.

So: B, unless you disagree — say A or C and I'll build that instead. Either way it is one component used from both pages.
