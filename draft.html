What it does today

Four faults, and they get worse with each partner. A headline turns into an inventory — the band exists to say what to do next, and a roll-call is a list to read instead of a decision made. It names everyone equally, so it tells you nothing about which of them you have actually left hanging. The names are already on the page, in the "Your partners" rail, with their topics — this is the second time you read them. And it cannot overflow gracefully: names are typed by people, there is no cap and no "+2 more", so the band's one line becomes three.

Trade-off. Safe, short, never breaks. But it is the least useful of the three: it tells you to do something without helping you decide anything.

Option B

Name the one you have left longest

Instead of listing everyone, lead with the partner you have gone longest without meeting, and let the rest be a count. This is the only option that makes a decision rather than describing a state — and the gap is a fact we already hold, since sessions carry a date and a room.

If you want my opinion

B, with A as its fallback

The band is the one place on Today that is supposed to tell you what to do next. A list of names is not that; the longest gap is. B also happens to fix the thing the current copy gets wrong — it stops saying "both" about four people — and it needs the fallback anyway, which is A, so building B gets you A for free.

If you would rather not touch the data layer today, A alone is a real improvement and about ten lines. I would skip C: it costs nearly the same as A and keeps the flaw.
