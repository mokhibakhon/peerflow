Where a partnership goes quiet

The first two are the same bug: somebody presses a button, the database does exactly the right thing, and the page comes back looking identical. Accept → first session and completed session → next session are the two biggest drops between signing up and studying weekly, and neither was a missing feature — they were presses that ended nowhere.

The third is a missing one. "I can't make Thursday" had nowhere to go but Cancel, so the difference between a clash and a breakup was left for the two people to sort out in chat, or not at all.

Everything below is the live markup and the live stylesheet, in the states the app now produces. Nothing is booked by anything here: every path still ends in a proposal the other person has to accept.

1 · Somebody accepts your request

The accept now writes a real notification to the person who asked — the same table every session event goes through, so it survives the tab closing and it is emailed like the rest. The link in it is app.html?plan=<their id>, which opens the booking form with them already in it.

Before: the row changed status. The person who asked found out the next time they happened to open PeerFlow and look at the bell, and the person who accepted was handed back the directory of strangers they had just stopped needing.

Both note triggers stand down during a move, so the partner is told once — "Amir moved your session. Was Monday 11:00, now Tuesday 17:00." — rather than being told it was cancelled and then, separately, that something new was proposed. The first of those two is alarming and obsolete by the time it is read, and both were emailed.

Reliability is untouched. Moving with six hours' notice or more already settles as cancelled_early, which the score does not count. Moving inside six hours still counts as a late cancellation, because your partner has lost the evening either way — so the form says the six-hour line out loud instead of a new outcome being invented to soften it.

5 · Nobody ever decided to stop

The quietest failure in the product. Two people match, they meet, it goes well — and then neither books the next one. No falling out, no no-show; the third session simply never gets proposed and a month later both assume the other lost interest.

Before: at seven days the band said "It's been nine days since you studied with Amir" — the right sentence in the wrong place, because it is only ever read by somebody who has opened PeerFlow. The half of the pair that needs reaching is the half that is not looking.

Fourteen days, and the band changes its question

It has been a while

You and Amir haven’t studied together recently.

Your last session was three weeks ago. Plan your next one — pick an hour and send it over, and it lands on both calendars once they accept.

Study with Amir again

Nothing in it says anybody should have done something, because nobody did anything wrong — that is asserted in the tests, along with there being no exclamation mark and no invitation to go and find somebody else. Not now is real: it stamps the partnership so neither of them is asked again for three weeks, rather than hiding a card until the next reload.

Fourteen days is read off the product, not picked. The default goal is two sessions a week; sinceWords() already starts counting in weeks at fourteen days; a standing slot books four weeks ahead, so a pair with one can never reach this state. The existing seven-day line is untouched — this is a second, quieter state above it.

And the person who is not here gets told. There is no scheduler behind a static site, so nudge_dormant() copies what settle_due() and send_due_reminders() already do: it runs when somebody opens the app and acts on the other person's rows. Only the other person is notified — telling the one reading the band would be telling them what their own screen says. One note per partnership per three weeks, however many times either of them opens anything.

What this did not touch

No new tables and no migration — the accept notification goes through notify_partner(), which has been in the database since the MVP migration and was called from nowhere. Attendance, reliability, the no-show policy, the standing-slot rules and messaging are all unchanged, and their tests were run to say so rather than assumed. Rescheduling adds one migration — supabase/migration-reschedule.sql — which has to be run by hand before the Reschedule button appears; until it is, the button is not drawn at all rather than drawn and refusing. Dormancy nudges and scheduled reminders are deliberately not here.
