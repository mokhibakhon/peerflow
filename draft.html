What a 404 page should do

Right now peerflow.dev/random — or any address that is not a file in the repo — answers with Vercel's own error page: black, an error code, a deployment id, and no link back to the site. Three ways to replace it, all with the real header and footer above and below. B is what is on the branch; A or C is a small edit.

A · Say it and get out of the way

not shipped

The whole page is the sentence and a way home. For: a 404 is not a destination, and a menu of links on one is a site trying to keep somebody who was going somewhere specific. It is also the only version with nothing in it to maintain. Against: it does nothing for the addresses that are one character from a real page — /privacy, /frontend — which are most of the 404s a site with no clean-URL rewrites actually gets.

B · Name the address, then guess at it

on the branch

Prints the address back so the typo is visible, offers the page it looks like when there is one, and lists four destinations in order of preference when there is not. For: the guess is the part that earns its place — /privacy, /frontend and /singup all land on a real page in one click instead of a dead end, and every other option here leaves those people to start again. Against: it is the only option with logic in it, so it is the only one that can be wrong; the page list it searches is hand-written, which is why dev/seo-tests.js asserts it covers every indexable page.

C · Answer with the eight paths

not shipped

Same hero and same guess as B, but the card underneath is the eight learning-path pages rather than four site destinations. For: the path pages are the public pages with inbound links, so a stale or mistyped link is more likely aimed at one of them than at the home page, and a visitor arriving on a broken link has not chosen a track yet — this is the choice the site wants them to make anyway. Against: a 404 that answers with a marketing grid reads as a landing page wearing an error message, and it is wrong for the signed-in half of the site: somebody who mistypes an app URL is offered eight things they already picked between.
