Which funnel?

Both options show the same seven numbers from funnel_counts(). Each is drawn at today's scale and at the scale a launch might produce, because the honest test is whether it survives both — and today's scale is the one the page has to work at first.

Nothing here is analytics. There is no script, no beacon, no page-view and no third party, and none is being added. privacy.html promises exactly that and the promise is worth more than a chart. Every number below is counted from rows PeerFlow already holds and already says it holds: the account you made, the profile you filled in, the sessions you booked.

It counts people, not events. A session is two rows, one per person, so counting rows would report two people for every meeting between two — and report two for a proposal nobody answered as readily as for a session both attended. Each step is the number of distinct people who ever reached it, so every step is a subset of the one above and the gaps are real drop-off.

Option A

The proportional funnel

What a funnel chart normally means: each bar as wide as its share of the top step. The shape carries the story and needs no reading.

Today — 11 accounts

After a launch — 340 accounts

Option B

The ladder

Every step gets the same room and its own number, the proportion moves into a bar inside the row, and the drop between two steps is printed rather than implied. The worst one is called out, because it is the only line on the page you can act on.

Today — 11 accounts

After a launch — 340 accounts

Decided: Option A

The owner picked A, and it is built — app-metrics.html, owner-only and linked from nowhere.

My recommendation had been B, and the argument for it is left standing above rather than quietly deleted, because it is the thing to re-read if the page ever stops being readable: below the second step every bar is a sliver, and at eleven accounts Sent a request, Has a partner and Proposed a session draw the same 137px bar three times.

Two things in the shipped page answer that without changing the form. The count sits outside each bar rather than inside it, since below about 40px a bar is narrower than its own number. And each row carries its share of the step above, which is what separates three bars that are otherwise identical. Both are ordinary funnel-chart furniture, not the ladder in disguise: the rows are still proportional and the drop is still read from the shape.

What did not survive is the colour idea. A light-to-dark ramp down the funnel cannot be built from the tokens this site declares — --p0 is 1.11:1 against a white card and --p1 1.45:1, both under the 2:1 floor where a fill stops being visible — so four steps are usable for a seven-step funnel. Every bar is the single accent green instead.
