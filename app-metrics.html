Metrics

Two things, kept apart on purpose. How far people get is counted from rows PeerFlow already holds — the account somebody made, the profile they filled in, the sessions they booked. Where visits come from is counted by a first-party beacon on the public pages, which records a page view and nothing that could join two of them together: no id, no cookie, no fingerprint, no third party. The privacy page says the same in the same words.

Where people get to

Each step is the number of people who ever reached it, so every one is a subset of the step above and the gaps are real drop-off. It counts people rather than sessions on purpose: a session is two rows, one per person, so counting rows would report two people for every meeting between two.

The last thirty days

Each column is one day. Its height is how many accounts were made; the green is how many of those went on to finish signing up. A launch shows up here as one tall column, and whether it was worth doing shows up as how much of that column is green.

Views by day

Thirty days, one column each. A day nobody visited is a zero rather than a gap, so a quiet week looks like a quiet week instead of a straight line drawn between the days either side of it.

Where visits came from

A campaign tag wins over the referring site when both are present, because somebody chose the tag and the browser chose the referrer. direct is an arrival with neither — typed in, or from an app that strips referrers. A large direct share is a finding rather than a gap: it means people already knew the name.

      Tagged campaigns

      Arrivals carrying a utm_ tag, grouped by all three fields. This is how one launch is told apart from another — add ?utm_source=producthunt&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=launch to the link you post, and it lands here instead of in the pile marked direct. A dash means that field was not set.

      Recent views

      The last fifty, newest first. Time is in your local time; their time is the same moment where the visitor was, which is the more useful of the two for a product that matches people across timezones. Two rows a second apart may be one person clicking through or two people arriving at once — there is no identifier here, so nothing can tell the difference. It is a log, not a profile.
