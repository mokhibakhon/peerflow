Study-partner compatibility checklist

Most study partnerships do not fail in week three because somebody lost interest. They fail because they were never compatible, and both people found out slowly instead of in the first ten minutes.

7 min readEight checks, and only three of them are about what you're learning

Matching on subject is the obvious part and the least predictive. Two people learning React at the same level, in the same timezone, can still produce nothing together — because one of them wants fifty silent minutes and the other wants somebody to explain hooks.

Here is what is actually worth checking, in the order it is worth checking it. The first three are mechanical and a platform can answer them for you. The last five are about people, and nobody can answer them except you.

The eight checks

Close enough in level that neither of you is teachingNot identical — a small gap is useful and each of you will be ahead on something. A large one turns the session into unpaid tutoring, and the person doing the teaching stops turning up.
At least two real hours you both actually haveNot “we’re both free evenings”. Two specific overlapping hours, so that when one week collapses you still have the other. One shared window is not a schedule, it is a single point of failure.
A timezone gap that survives the calendarThree or four hours is fine and often better — their morning is your evening and both of you are fresh. Eight is not, and it gets worse when one of you changes clocks in October and the other does not.
The same idea of what a session isHeads-down parallel work, or company while doing light reading? Both are legitimate and they do not mix. This is the single most common mismatch and almost nobody asks about it before the first session.
The same idea of what you want from itAccountability, or help? If one of you wants a witness and the other wants a tutor, the arrangement is quietly lopsided from the first week and will end with somebody feeling used.
A slot that survives six weeks, not oneAsk what their next two months look like. A time that works during their holiday and not during their term is a partnership with an expiry date already on it.
They replied before you ever metHow somebody handles the messages before the first session is the best available predictor of how they will handle the third one. Slow and vague now is slow and absent later.
You would be willing to tell them you did nothing this weekThe real test, and the one to keep if you drop the other seven. If admitting you fell behind would be embarrassing enough to make you cancel instead, this person cannot hold you accountable — they can only make you avoid them.

The first three are answered before you meet. PeerFlow matches on path, level and the hours you genuinely share, so the only things left to check are the human ones.

The three mechanical ones, in more detail

These are worth understanding even when something else checks them for you, because the reason they matter is not obvious from the check itself.

Level. The instinct is to look for somebody ahead of you, and it is the wrong instinct. Somebody ahead of you is a mentor, and mentoring is a favour that runs out. The partnership that lasts is between two people who are each occasionally useful to the other — and that only happens near the same level, where being stuck is mutual.

Shared hours. A shared window in your head is a band — "we're both free Tuesday evening". A shared window in practice is an hour that exists in both of your actual lives, in both of your actual timezones, after work and before sleep. Those are different things, and the gap between them is where most first sessions are never booked.

Your partner's evening is somebody's 5am. PeerFlow intersects the two of you as real intervals rather than matching the word "evening" against the word "evening", so the times it offers are hours you can both genuinely make.

Timezone. Some distance is an advantage. A partner four hours ahead is finishing their day as you start yours, which means neither of you is asking the other to give up their best hours. The failure case is not distance, it is distance so large that the only overlapping hour is bad for both of you — an hour nobody wants gets cancelled the first time anything else comes up.

The five nobody checks

The mechanical checks get all the attention because they are answerable. The five below decide whether the partnership lasts, and every one of them can be settled in one honest message before the first session.

Send something like this, and read the reply carefully:

Before the first session I'm after quiet parallel work, not tutoring — I say what I'm doing, we both go heads-down, we compare notes at the break. Does that match what you want? And realistically, can you keep Tuesday 19:00 for the next couple of months?

Two questions, and between them they cover checks four, five and six. Somebody who answers both directly has also answered check seven.

What to do when it isn't a match

Say so in week one. This is the part everybody gets wrong, and it is understandable — nobody wants to tell a stranger who has been perfectly pleasant that this is not working.

But the alternative is worse for both of you. The usual path is that sessions get quietly rescheduled, then one gets missed, then a week passes with nothing said, and eventually both people are relieved. That is five weeks of somebody's learning spent on an arrangement that was finished in week one, and it leaves both of you slightly more reluctant to try again.

One message ends it cleanly: the times aren't working, or you're after a different kind of session, and you hope they find someone. Nobody has been insulted, and both of you get the weeks back.

A partnership ending is not a failure of the format. The first match working is the exception, not the rule — which is an argument for making the first session short, and for checking these eight things before you build a habit around somebody.

Start short

Book twenty-five minutes with somebody new rather than fifty. It is long enough to find out whether working next to this particular person feels like working, and short enough that neither of you has risked an evening on it. If it went well, book the next one before you leave the call.

The eight checks above take about five minutes and one message. A partnership that survives them is worth more than four that did not get checked.
