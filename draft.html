The band after a session, and what the rail is for

The two things I left out of the dashboard pass, because both are decisions rather than clean-ups. First: check-out now exists, and the band ignores it. Second: the rail is four cards deep and offers a power feature to people who have not had a session yet. Nothing here is live — the dashboard still behaves the way the "Now" panels below show.

One — the band after a session has ended

Now

It asks you to book while it is still waiting to be told what happened

Check-out is live: a session whose end time has passed asks whether it happened, and the answer is the first thing ever written to sessions.attended. But it asks from the card, and the band above carries on as though the session never took place. The band is the one element whose job is "what to do now", and here the thing to do now is answer a question about Friday — not book Tuesday.

Option A

The band asks, and it is the only thing it asks

A seventh tone, slotted below "your room is open" and "needs your answer" and above everything else. It holds one session — the most recent — and the card keeps any others.

One question

Did Friday happen?

You and Asalxon had 50 minutes booked at 7:00 PM. Nobody has said either way.

Only your own answer — Asalxon is asked separately about theirs.

Trade-off. It is the honest reading of what the band is for, and it is two taps to clear. But it is retrospective, and it pushes "book your next session" down for as long as you ignore it — a band that nags about last week is a band you learn to scroll past. It also has to cap: three un-answered sessions cannot become three bands.

Option B

The band stays on what is next, and mentions it underneath

The footnote line the band already has — the one that says when the call link opens — carries the question instead, as a link down to the card.

Nothing booked

Pick an hour you’re both free — it lands on both calendars once they accept.

Trade-off. Nothing gets pushed down and the band keeps facing forwards, which is where a dashboard should face. But the footnote is the quietest line in the band, it is grey on green, and asking for the one piece of data the whole reliability score depends on in the smallest type on the page is how it stays unanswered.

Option C

A strip above the band, not a tone inside it

Its own row, sitting between the top bar and the band. One line, two buttons, and it disappears the moment it is answered.

Friday, 7:00 PM with Asalxon. Did it happen?

Nothing booked

Book your next session with Asalxon.

Pick an hour you’re both free — it lands on both calendars once they accept.

Propose a time

Trade-off. Both things get said at full volume and neither displaces the other, and several unanswered sessions stack as several thin rows rather than fighting over one band. The cost is a new piece of furniture above the element that is supposed to be the top of the page — and the band stops being the first thing you read, which is the one rule this dashboard has held to so far.

If you want my opinion

C, and A if you would rather not add a row

The question is worth asking properly — it is the only source of the attendance data, the reliability score is dark without it, and the conduct page implies people can see who turns up. B asks properly but quietly, which is the same as not asking. Between A and C: A is cheaper and reuses a tone, C is honest that these are two different kinds of thing and scales to more than one. I would take C, and I would make it disappear for good once answered rather than nagging on the next load.

Now

Four cards, and one of them is offered too early

The rail runs about a thousand pixels — taller than the main column now that the sessions list collapses, so the booked state has a short left side and a long right one. Trade-off. Two cards instead of four, and the two halves of "how am I doing" finally sit together. But the twelve-week plan is the only forward-looking thing on the dashboard and folding it under a streak ring makes it a footnote to a counter — and the counter is about turning up, not about what you are learning.

If you want my opinion

A, and leave the length alone

A fixes a real thing — offering a standing slot to someone with no sessions is selling a habit to a person who has not started — and it costs two conditions. B is tidier and I do not think it is right: the plan is the only part of this page about the subject rather than the scheduling, and it should not become a line inside a streak card. The short-left long-right imbalance is worth living with; it is the honest shape of a page whose main column is empty because you have nothing booked, and the fix for that is having something booked.
