What the calendar leaves out

Re-checking the reschedule work turned up two things in calHourWindow() and the filter that feeds it. The first was losing a booking off the screen and is fixed. The second is a question about what the grid is for, and it is yours to answer.

Fixed

A proposal that was not on the calendar at all

The hour window is built from the earliest and latest hour the week contains, then capped at fourteen rows as a backstop against drawing a full day. The cap used to be hi = lo + 13 — it trimmed the bottom of the window without looking at what was in it. A week holding a 5am booking and a Friday 7pm proposal drew 05:00 to 18:00 and never rendered the Friday cell. The sessions card said the proposal existed; the calendar beside it showed a free evening.

This is the same divergence between the calendar and the session list that the reschedule flow exists to prevent, arriving from the other direction — and it was invisible, because a cell that was never drawn looks exactly like an hour nothing is booked in.

Empty availability rows are what the cap may take. If the bookings themselves span more than fourteen hours the window stays wider than the cap — the card scrolls internally, so a long window costs a scroll and a short one costs a booking.

Needs a decision

Should a session you have already had stay on the calendar?

Right now it does not. calSessions keeps only confirmed and proposed, so the moment settle_due() flips a finished session to completed it disappears from the grid — mid-week, without anything happening. Page back a week and the calendar is empty even though you studied on the Tuesday.

What makes this a question rather than a bug: .cal-ev.done is fully styled in app.css — grey block, grey bar, soft text — and is currently almost unreachable, which reads like it was meant to be drawn. But the legend under the grid has three keys and none of them is "done", which reads like it was not. Both are in the shipped code, so the intent has to come from you.

The calendar stays a picture of what is ahead of you. History lives on the sessions card and the person page, which both already list it.
