Badge earned: the bug, and three ways to celebrate it

Two separate things here. The first is a fault and is already fixed on the branch. The second is a judgement call and is why this page exists — press the buttons, they are the real popup, not a picture of one.

1. The popup was showing the locked badge

Seven of the twelve badges shipped a grey, padlocked hero drawing, and the popup always loads the hero. So the moment you earned “Five sessions”, you were shown the version that means you have not earned this. The five one-shot badges were fine, which is why it went unnoticed — it is exactly the seven counting badges that were wrong.

What you sawgrey, padlocked — the locked art
What you see nowthe earned badge, in its own colour
Ten hoursviolet, as the palette always intended
Six weeksamber

The seven were rebuilt from each badge’s own earned drawing by the same rule the five correct ones follow — proven by regenerating those five and getting a byte-for-byte match before touching the broken ones.

2. Now make it feel like something

One rule shapes all three: the words stay flat. The house voice does not congratulate, and “Badge earned” is the right register — so the light and the motion carry the feeling instead of the copy. Every particle is tinted from the badge’s own colour rather than a generic confetti rainbow, so earning Ten sessions feels different from earning Six weeks. All three stop; none loop. All three collapse to a plain fade under prefers-reduced-motion.

A

Burst

A ray burst opens behind the badge and turns as it fades, with a short confetti throw over the top. The most literally celebratory of the three.

Reads instantly as “achievement”. The risk is that it is the loudest thing in an app that is otherwise quiet — the twelfth time you see it may be one too many.

B

Bloom

No particles. Three halo rings push out in the badge’s colour and a highlight sweeps across the face, as if the light caught it. The badge is the whole event.

The most grown-up, and the one that would still feel right on the fortieth badge. It asks the artwork to do the work, which — now that the artwork is correct — it can.

C

Stamp

The badge drops in with real weight, the card gives under it, and confetti falls from the top edge the way something lands. Physical rather than pyrotechnic.

Between the two. The weight is what sells it; the confetti is a garnish and could be dropped entirely if you want it quieter still.

My pick is B, with C’s weighted entrance folded into it — the halo and sweep for the light, the stamp for the arrival, and no confetti at all. It celebrates without spending goodwill, and it is the only one of the three that does not get tiring. Say a letter, or “B with C’s drop”, and I will put it on the real popup.
